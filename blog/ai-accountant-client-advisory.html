4 AI Prompts That Help CPAs Deliver Better Client Advisory Work

July 30, 2026 6 min read CPAs, Bookkeepers, and Accounting Professionals

Advisory work is where accountants build long-term client value — and where time constraints most often get in the way. A client could benefit from a quarterly check-in, a proactive tax-saving note, or a clear year-end summary, but writing it from scratch for every client takes hours that don't bill at an advisory rate.

These four prompts let you produce personalized advisory communications in a fraction of the time. You supply the numbers and the specific context; AI handles the structure and the prose. You review and send.

Accuracy matters here. Every prompt contains specific financial numbers and tax guidance — always verify the output against your actual client data and current-year IRS guidance before sending. The prompts are designed to reject AI-invented numbers, but double-check the output every time.

Prompt 1 — Quarterly Business Review Email

A QBR email keeps clients connected to their own numbers and positions you as a proactive advisor rather than a year-end filer. This prompt writes one from your quarterly data input.

5 minutes
Copy and paste this prompt
Write a quarterly business review email for a small business client. Client name: [NAME]. Business name: [BUSINESS]. Quarter: [Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4 YEAR]. Revenue this quarter: $[AMOUNT] vs. last quarter: $[AMOUNT] vs. same quarter last year: $[AMOUNT or "not available"]. Expenses this quarter: $[AMOUNT]. Net income this quarter: $[AMOUNT]. Key financial observations (list what stands out — do not invent): [e.g., "revenue up 18% QoQ driven by new contracts" / "expenses up due to staffing increase" / "profit margin compressed vs. same period last year"]. One recommended action or item to discuss: [DESCRIBE OR WRITE "none this quarter"]. Meeting offer: [YES — offer to schedule a call | NO — update only]. Write a 200–250 word email that (1) opens with the main financial headline for the quarter, not "I hope this email finds you well," (2) presents the numbers in plain language with brief context for any significant change, (3) mentions the recommended action if there is one, (4) ends with a clear next step. Do not add analysis or numbers I have not provided.

Aim to send these within the first week of the new quarter while the data is fresh. Clients who receive regular quarterly emails are significantly less likely to churn — and more likely to take up advisory services.

Prompt 2 — Tax-Saving Recommendation Letter

This prompt writes a proactive tax planning note — the kind of communication that turns a compliance client into an advisory client. Use it when you spot a legitimate savings opportunity in a client's financials.

5 minutes
Copy and paste this prompt
Write a tax-saving recommendation letter for a client. Client name: [NAME]. Business entity type: [sole proprietor / S-corp / LLC / C-corp / partnership]. Tax year in question: [YEAR]. Recommendation: [DESCRIBE THE SPECIFIC OPPORTUNITY — e.g., "converting from sole prop to S-corp could reduce self-employment tax at your current income level" / "funding a SEP-IRA before the filing deadline could reduce taxable income by up to $X" / "accelerating Q4 deductible expenses into this tax year"]. Estimated benefit: [$AMOUNT or "to be calculated after review" if not yet determined]. Action required by: [DATE OR "discuss on our next call"]. Requirements: (1) frame this as a discussion to explore, not a guarantee of savings — include language like "depending on your specific situation" where appropriate, (2) recommend they schedule a call to review the details before taking action, (3) do not add tax law citations I have not provided — leave those for our call, (4) keep tone professional and specific, not salesy. Under 200 words.

The key is specificity. "You might be able to save money on taxes" is noise. "Based on your Q3 numbers, converting to an S-corp election could reduce self-employment tax by approximately $X at your current income — worth a 20-minute call before year-end" is an action item.

Prompt 3 — Year-End Advisory Summary

A year-end summary positions you ahead of filing season and gives clients something concrete to review and discuss. This prompt writes a structured summary from your annual data.

6 minutes
Copy and paste this prompt
Write a year-end advisory summary email for a small business client. Client: [NAME]. Business: [BUSINESS NAME]. Year: [YEAR]. Full-year revenue: $[AMOUNT]. Full-year expenses: $[AMOUNT]. Net income: $[AMOUNT]. Estimated tax liability (if calculated): $[AMOUNT or "TBD pending final review"]. Key financial events or changes this year: [LIST — e.g., "hired 2 employees," "expanded to a second location," "revenue grew 24% YoY"]. Filing deadlines coming up: [LIST — e.g., "1099-NEC deadline Jan 31," "Q4 estimated tax due Jan 15," "partnership return April 15"]. Items to discuss before filing: [DESCRIBE OR WRITE "none outstanding"]. Write a 250–300 word email that (1) opens with the most important headline about their year in financial terms, (2) covers the full-year numbers with brief context for notable changes, (3) lists upcoming deadlines clearly, (4) flags anything that needs discussion before we file, (5) invites them to schedule a year-end review call. Professional and direct — not a newsletter, not a form letter.

Send this in early December, not January. Getting clients to act before year-end is where most advisory value lives. By January, most planning opportunities have already closed.

Prompt 4 — New Client Onboarding Welcome Email

A clear, organized onboarding email sets the tone for the entire client relationship. This prompt writes a welcome email that explains what happens next, what you need from them, and how to reach you.

4 minutes
Copy and paste this prompt
Write a new client onboarding welcome email for an accounting practice. Client name: [NAME]. Business name: [BUSINESS]. Services engaged: [LIST — e.g., "monthly bookkeeping," "quarterly tax planning," "annual business return"]. First step the client needs to take: [DESCRIBE — e.g., "share QuickBooks access via the link I sent separately" / "upload your last 2 years' returns to our secure portal" / "schedule your onboarding call at: [LINK]"]. Onboarding call scheduled: [YES — date/time | NO — still to be scheduled]. Primary contact at the firm: [NAME, email, phone]. Document sharing method: [DESCRIBE OR WRITE "we will set this up on our onboarding call"]. Write a 150–200 word email that (1) warmly confirms we are glad to have them as a client, (2) sets clear expectations for the first 2 weeks, (3) makes the first required action completely clear, (4) tells them exactly who to contact with questions. Do not include a list of every service in fine detail — one clear next step is more effective than a complete overview at this stage.

The goal of the first email is not to impress the client with everything you do — it is to get them to complete one action that starts the workflow. Keep it short and move them to the first step.
