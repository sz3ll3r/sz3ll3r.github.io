40 AI Prompts Every CPA Should Be Using in 2026

July 1, 2026 8 min read CPAs, Bookkeepers, Accounting Professionals

If you are a solo CPA or bookkeeper, a significant portion of your non-billable time goes to writing. Monthly financial narratives. Tax season document requests. Engagement letters. Status update emails. Meeting follow-ups. Advisory memos.

None of that is what you were trained to do. And none of it requires your professional judgment. It just requires a competent first draft — which is exactly what AI does well.

This article covers the 8 highest-value prompts from the 40-prompt guide I built for accounting professionals. Each one replaces 10–60 minutes of blank-screen time with a 30-second copy-paste operation.

Important: Every AI output is a draft. You review it, edit it, and approve it. You are always the licensed professional responsible for the final work. These prompts do not constitute tax or legal advice.

1. Monthly Financial Summary Narrative saves 45-60 min

The single most time-consuming deliverable for most accounting professionals: translating the numbers into something a business owner without an accounting background can actually understand. This prompt takes the raw data and produces a client-ready narrative in 2–3 paragraphs.

Copy-paste promptYou are an accounting professional writing a monthly financial summary for a small business client. Use the following financial data to write a clear, professional narrative (2–3 paragraphs) that a business owner without an accounting background can understand. Avoid jargon. Focus on what the numbers mean for the business. Client type: [INDUSTRY — e.g., restaurant, dental practice, retail] Month: [MONTH YEAR] Revenue: $[AMOUNT] ([UP/DOWN] [%] vs. last month) Operating expenses: $[AMOUNT] ([UP/DOWN] [%] vs. last month) Net income: $[AMOUNT] Key variances: [LIST 2–3 line items that changed significantly] Notable one-time items: [DESCRIBE OR "none"] Write the narrative now.
Example output (AI draft)

"October was a solid month for your practice. Revenue reached $47,200, up 8% from September, driven primarily by an increase in new patient appointments following the back-to-school season. Operating expenses came in at $31,800, roughly in line with last month, though your supply costs rose $1,400 — something worth monitoring heading into Q4..."

2. Budget vs. Actual Variance Explanation saves 30-45 min

Explaining variances in plain English is where most accountants either write too much (over-explaining) or too little (confusing the client). This prompt hits the right level.

Copy-paste promptWrite a professional variance analysis narrative for a client financial report. The tone should be informative, not alarming. Explain why variances occurred when possible, and suggest what the client should watch in the coming month. Budget period: [MONTH/QUARTER, YEAR] Total revenue — Budget: $[X], Actual: $[X], Variance: $[X] ([FAVORABLE/UNFAVORABLE]) Total expenses — Budget: $[X], Actual: $[X], Variance: $[X] ([FAVORABLE/UNFAVORABLE]) Top 3 variance drivers: 1. [LINE ITEM]: Budget $[X], Actual $[X] — [BRIEF REASON IF KNOWN] 2. [LINE ITEM]: Budget $[X], Actual $[X] — [BRIEF REASON IF KNOWN] 3. [LINE ITEM]: Budget $[X], Actual $[X] — [BRIEF REASON IF KNOWN] Write a 2–3 paragraph narrative for inclusion in a client report.

3. Tax Season Document Request Email saves 15-20 min

You send this email dozens of times in Q1. The version most CPAs send is either too long (clients ignore it) or too short (you get a trickle of documents over 6 weeks). This prompt produces a version that is friendly, specific, and includes a numbered checklist.

Copy-paste promptWrite a professional, friendly email to an individual tax client requesting the documents we need to prepare their [TAX YEAR] federal and [STATE] state tax return. The tone should be helpful, not demanding. Include a clear numbered list of documents. Client situation: [W-2 employee / self-employed / rental property owner / investment income / sold a home / major life event: DESCRIBE] Preferred method to send documents: [CLIENT PORTAL / EMAIL / SECURE UPLOAD LINK / DROP OFF] Deadline for receiving documents: [DATE] Our firm name: [NAME] Write the email now.

4. Extension Notification Email saves 10-15 min

Clients panic when they hear "extension." This prompt produces a calm, reassuring version that explains exactly what an extension means and what they need to do.

Copy-paste promptWrite a professional email notifying a tax client that we are filing an extension on their behalf. Reassure them this is normal and does not mean they owe more or that there is a problem. Client name: [FIRST NAME] Return type: [Personal 1040 / Business S-Corp / Partnership / Other] Original deadline: [DATE] Extended deadline: [DATE] Estimated tax due: [AMOUNT or "we estimate you do not owe additional tax"] Payment required by original deadline: [YES — $AMOUNT / NO] Write the email now.

5. Engagement Letter — Tax Preparation saves 60-90 min

Writing an engagement letter from scratch is painful. This prompt produces a complete professional draft — scope, responsibilities, fee, payment terms — that you edit and sign rather than draft from a blank page. Always have your final version reviewed against your professional liability carrier's template.

Copy-paste promptDraft a professional tax preparation engagement letter. Include the scope of services, client responsibilities, our firm's responsibilities, fee basis, and a disclaimer that this is not legal advice. Client name: [INDIVIDUAL NAME or BUSINESS NAME, ENTITY TYPE] Services: Preparation of [TAX YEAR] federal [Form 1040 / 1120S / 1065] and [STATE] state return Fee basis: [FLAT FEE of $X / HOURLY at $X/hr] Client responsibilities: Provide complete and accurate information Firm responsibilities: Prepare the return based on information provided Payment terms: [DUE UPON COMPLETION / 50% DEPOSIT UPFRONT] Firm name: [NAME] Preparer credential: [CPA/EA/other] Write the engagement letter. Use [DATE] and [CLIENT SIGNATURE LINE] placeholders.

6. Management Letter — Internal Control Finding saves 20-30 min

Writing up control findings in the right format — condition, criteria, effect, recommendation — is a learned skill. This prompt gets the structure right every time.

Copy-paste promptWrite a management letter paragraph describing an internal control deficiency for a small business client. State the condition found, the criteria (what proper controls look like), the potential effect, and the recommendation. Control area: [CASH / PAYROLL / ACCOUNTS PAYABLE / ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE / OTHER] Condition: [DESCRIBE WHAT YOU FOUND] Risk: [DESCRIBE THE POTENTIAL PROBLEM] Recommendation: [DESCRIBE THE CONTROL YOU RECOMMEND] Tone: Professional, constructive, not alarming Write the paragraph now.

7. Year-End Tax Planning Memo saves 30-45 min

Clients who receive a proactive year-end planning memo stay longer and refer more. Most CPAs know this and still don't send one because writing it takes too long. This prompt changes that.

Copy-paste promptWrite a professional year-end tax planning memo. Summarize the client's current tax position, highlight 2–3 opportunities to reduce their tax bill before [DATE], and explain next steps clearly. Client type: [INDIVIDUAL / S-CORP / PARTNERSHIP / SOLE PROPRIETOR] Tax year: [YEAR] Estimated taxable income: $[X] Estimated tax liability: $[X] Planning opportunities: 1. [OPPORTUNITY — e.g., "Maximize SEP-IRA contribution by Dec 31 — estimated savings $X"] 2. [OPPORTUNITY] 3. [OPPORTUNITY] Deadline for action: [DATE] Keep it under 350 words. Frame as options to discuss, not specific tax advice.

8. Difficult Client Email Response saves 20-30 min

Everyone has a client who sends an angry or confused email at the worst possible time. The blank screen when you're trying to write back professionally — without being defensive or over-apologetic — is a specific kind of awful. This prompt solves it.

Copy-paste promptI received the following email from a client and need help writing a professional, de-escalating response. I want to acknowledge their concern, explain the situation clearly without being defensive, and propose a clear next step. The client's email: [PASTE CLIENT EMAIL HERE] My firm's situation / the facts: [EXPLAIN WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED] What I want to offer or resolve: [DESCRIBE YOUR PROPOSED RESOLUTION] Tone: Professional, empathetic, solution-focused Write the response email now.

How Many Hours Could This Save You?

The 8 prompts above cover the most time-consuming recurring writing tasks. If you use even 5 of them consistently:

  • Monthly financial narratives: 45 minutes x 10 clients = 7.5 hours saved per month
  • Tax season document request emails: 15 minutes x 30 clients = 7.5 hours saved per Q1
  • Engagement letters: 60 minutes x however many new clients you take on
  • Year-end planning memos: 30 minutes x every client who gets one

For a solo practitioner with 50 active clients, consistent use of AI drafting tools is worth 5–10 billable hours per month in recovered time. At even $100/hour, that's $500–$1,000 in capacity per month.
