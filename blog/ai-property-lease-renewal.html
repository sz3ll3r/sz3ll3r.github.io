4 AI Prompts That Take the Stress Out of Lease Renewal Season

July 30, 2026 6 min read Property Managers

Lease renewal season means sending dozens of letters at once — each one slightly different based on the tenant, the property, the rent increase, and your state's notice requirements. It is one of those tasks that is technically simple and completely tedious at scale.

AI handles the variation well. You give it the tenant's details and your specific numbers; it writes the letter. These four prompts cover the main renewal scenarios: offering a renewal, explaining a rent increase, sending a non-renewal notice, and handling the tenant who pushes back.

Important: Lease-related notices are legally significant documents. The prompts produce solid drafts — always verify the output against your state's landlord-tenant statute before sending. Cure periods, required notice language, and timing requirements vary by jurisdiction. When in doubt, have your attorney review the first version for any new notice type.

Prompt 1 — Lease Renewal Offer Letter

The standard renewal offer. Works for renewals at the same rent, renewals with a small increase, and lease term changes (e.g., moving from 12-month to month-to-month).

4 minutes
Copy and paste this prompt
Write a professional lease renewal offer letter for a residential property. Property address: [ADDRESS]. Tenant name(s): [NAMES]. Current lease end date: [DATE]. Renewal offer: [same rent / rent increasing from $X to $Y per month]. New term: [12-month lease / month-to-month / other]. New lease start date: [DATE]. Response deadline: [DATE — typically 30 days before end date, check your state]. Include: (1) a warm but professional opening thanking the tenant for their tenancy, (2) clear statement of the renewal offer and any rent change, (3) the response deadline and what the tenant needs to do (sign and return / contact the office), (4) what happens if they don't respond by the deadline. Tone: professional and matter-of-fact. Do not add clauses or requirements I have not listed. Flag any bracket I left unfilled. Keep under 200 words.

Keep the letter short. Long renewal letters with unnecessary legal language make tenants anxious and slow down the response. The goal is a clear offer and a clear deadline.

Prompt 2 — Rent Increase Explanation

Tenants who understand why rent is going up are less likely to move out over it. This prompt writes an explanation that's honest and matter-of-fact without being defensive or overly apologetic.

4 minutes
Copy and paste this prompt
Write a short explanation of a rent increase for a residential tenant. Current rent: $[AMOUNT]. New rent: $[AMOUNT]. Increase amount: $[AMOUNT] per month. Reason(s) for increase (choose what applies and delete the rest): [property tax increase / insurance cost increase / maintenance cost increases / market rate alignment / inflation / utility cost increases / other: specify]. Effective date: [DATE]. Notice period given: [X days, confirm this meets your state minimum]. Write this as 2–3 short paragraphs the property manager can include with the renewal letter or send separately. Tone: transparent and factual — not apologetic, not defensive, not dismissive of the impact. Acknowledge the increase is real, briefly explain what drove it, and close with an invitation to contact the office with questions. Do not suggest the tenant comparison-shop other rentals. Under 120 words.

Avoid the two failure modes: over-explaining in a way that reads as guilty, or saying nothing and sounding indifferent. A two-sentence factual explanation — "Property taxes increased 12% this year. This increase covers part of that cost." — is almost always better than a paragraph of caveats.

Prompt 3 — Non-Renewal Notice

When you're not offering renewal — because of lease violations, a planned sale, or just a business decision — you still need to give proper notice. This prompt writes a notice that is clear and legally careful without being confrontational.

5 minutes
Copy and paste this prompt
Write a non-renewal notice for a residential lease. Property address: [ADDRESS]. Tenant name(s): [NAMES]. Current lease end date: [DATE]. Notice given date: [TODAY'S DATE]. Required notice period in this state: [X days — verify your state's minimum]. Reason for non-renewal (if you choose to state one): [planned sale of property / owner move-in / lease violation history / business decision — not required to specify in most states; delete this line if you prefer not to state a reason]. Move-out deadline: [DATE — must match lease end date or later per state law]. Include: (1) formal notice that the lease will not be renewed, (2) move-out date, (3) brief note on what happens with the security deposit (inspect after move-out, returned per state law timeline), (4) offer to answer questions. Tone: professional and neutral — not hostile, not warm. This is a business communication. Under 180 words. Flag any unfilled bracket before I finalize.

Do not add language about why you think the tenant should leave or anything that could read as harassment. Factual, professional, and complete. Check your state's required notice period before sending — failure to give proper notice can reset the clock.

Prompt 4 — Response to a Tenant Negotiating the Renewal Terms

Some tenants push back — they want a lower rent increase, a shorter term, or a delay in the effective date. This prompt helps you respond clearly without either giving away the store or starting a fight.

4 minutes
Copy and paste this prompt
Write a response to a tenant who has pushed back on their lease renewal terms. What they asked for: [e.g., reduce the rent increase from $150 to $75 / extend the response deadline by 2 weeks / change the lease term from 12 months to 6 months]. Your position (choose one): [A — you can meet them partway: describe the compromise | B — you cannot change the terms and the original offer stands]. If A: describe the compromise clearly and state the updated deadline for them to accept. If B: acknowledge their request respectfully, state clearly that the terms cannot be changed, and give a final deadline to accept or vacate. In both cases: (1) keep a professional and non-confrontational tone, (2) do not make promises about future rent if this is a one-year deal, (3) end with a clear next step. Under 150 words.

Use this to respond in writing — it creates a record of what was offered and what was decided. If a verbal conversation already happened, this prompt helps you confirm it in writing immediately after.
