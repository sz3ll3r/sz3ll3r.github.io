3 AI Prompts That Handle the Hardest Part of Property Management: Move-Outs

July 2026 7 min read Property Management

Move-outs create more disputes than any other part of property management. The inspection summary that is too vague to stand up in a deposit dispute. The deduction letter that sounds accusatory. The reference you promised to write but have been putting off for two weeks.

These three prompts handle the move-out process end-to-end — from inspection to deposit return to tenant reference — in professional, defensible language that takes under two minutes to produce.

Important: Security deposit rules vary by jurisdiction. These prompts produce professional communications, not legal advice. Always verify your local notice period, deduction documentation requirements, and return deadlines before sending. When in doubt, run the letter by your letting agent or solicitor.

Prompt 1 — Move-Out Inspection Summary (for the Tenant)

A clear, itemised inspection summary sent to the tenant immediately after a move-out walk-through does two things: it creates a documented record, and it reduces the element of surprise when the deposit decision arrives. Tenants who receive a clear summary upfront are significantly less likely to dispute the final outcome.

90 seconds
Copy this promptWrite a professional move-out inspection summary letter to a tenant. Details: - Tenant name: [TENANT NAME] - Property address: [ADDRESS] - Move-out date: [DATE] - Inspection date: [DATE] - Items found during inspection (list what was found): [e.g., scuff marks on living room wall, broken blind in bedroom 2, carpet stain in hallway, oven not cleaned to move-in standard, missing light bulb in bathroom] - Items within normal wear and tear (list): [e.g., minor paint fade, small nail holes from picture hanging] Format as a professional letter. Clearly distinguish between items that constitute normal wear and tear (no charge) and items that represent damage or cleaning shortfalls (may result in deduction). Use neutral, factual language throughout — not accusatory. State that the tenant will receive a deposit decision within [X days / 10 working days] along with supporting photographs. Include the landlord/agency name [NAME] and contact details [EMAIL / PHONE].

Prompt 2 — Security Deposit Deduction Letter

This is the communication tenants remember — and the one most likely to result in a dispute if it is vague, aggressive, or poorly reasoned. This prompt produces a clear, itemised letter that explains each deduction with its evidence basis, which is both the professional standard and the legal requirement in most jurisdictions.

2 minutes
Copy this promptWrite a professional security deposit deduction letter to a former tenant. Details: - Tenant name: [TENANT NAME] - Property address: [ADDRESS] - Original deposit amount: [AMOUNT] - Total deductions: [AMOUNT] - Breakdown of deductions (list each item, amount, and reason): [e.g., Professional carpet cleaning: £150 — carpet not returned to move-in condition; Repainting living room wall: £200 — damage beyond normal wear and tear; Replacement blind bedroom 2: £45 — broken, not repairable] - Items not deducted (include a brief normal wear-and-tear note to show good faith): [e.g., minor scuffs on kitchen floor — normal wear, no charge] - Deposit return amount: [AMOUNT] - Return timeline: within [X] days by [method: bank transfer / cheque] - Evidence available: [photographs taken at check-in and check-out / receipts / contractor invoices] Tone: professional, factual, firm but not hostile. Do not use accusatory language. Include clear itemisation so the tenant understands exactly what was charged and why. Mention that supporting evidence (photos and receipts) is available on request. Include landlord/agency name [NAME] and contact details [EMAIL / PHONE].

Prompt 3 — Tenant Reference Letter (Good Tenants)

Property managers who write good references get more requests for references from good tenants — which is actually a low-effort way to build a network. This prompt produces a solid reference in under a minute that covers the points prospective landlords and letting agents actually read: payment history, property care, and whether you would rent to them again.

60 seconds
Copy this promptWrite a professional landlord or letting agent reference letter for a former tenant. Details: - Tenant name: [TENANT NAME] - Property address: [ADDRESS] - Tenancy dates: [START DATE] to [END DATE] ([X years / months]) - Monthly rent: [AMOUNT] - Payment history: [e.g., always paid on time / one late payment in [X] months, resolved promptly] - Property condition at handover: [e.g., returned in excellent condition with professional cleaning / returned in good condition with minor wear] - Noise/neighbour complaints during tenancy: [None / brief description if any] - Would you rent to this tenant again? [Yes / Yes without hesitation] - Any other notable positives: [optional — e.g., reported maintenance issues promptly, communicated professionally throughout tenancy] Format as a formal letter dated today. Include your name, title, and contact details at the bottom. Keep to one page. Professional, factual tone — not gushing, but clearly positive.

Why move-outs need better communication, not just better documentation

Most deposit disputes come down to a communication gap, not an evidence gap. The inspection photographs exist. The cost breakdown is fair. But the tenant feels blindsided because they never received a clear summary of what was found, and the deduction letter arrived 3 weeks later worded like a legal threat.

These prompts do not solve the documentation problem — you still need timestamped photos, a signed check-in report, and itemised receipts. What they solve is the communication problem: clear language, consistent structure, professional tone, delivered promptly.
