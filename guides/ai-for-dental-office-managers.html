Prompt 1 · Patient recall

Bring overdue patients back without sounding pushy

Use when: reaching out to patients who haven't booked their 6-month cleaning.

Write a friendly, professional email to a dental patient who is 3 months
overdue for their 6-month cleaning. The practice name is [PRACTICE NAME].
The patient's first name is [NAME]. Keep it warm but not pushy. Include a
clear call to action to call or book online. Do not mention specific dental
problems or assume the patient has issues. Keep under 150 words.

Why it works: it bans the two things that make recall emails fail — guilt and assumed problems — so the message stays warm and gets replies instead of unsubscribes.

Prompt 2 · HIPAA-safe

Respond to a negative Google review without breaking HIPAA

Use when: a public review needs a reply and you can't confirm the person was ever a patient.

Write a HIPAA-compliant response to a negative Google review for a dental
practice. The reviewer said: "[PASTE REVIEW TEXT HERE]". Do NOT confirm or
deny the person was a patient. Do NOT reference any treatment details. Show
that we take concerns seriously, invite them to call us directly to discuss,
and provide a phone number [PHONE]. Professional and empathetic tone.
Under 100 words.

The trap it avoids: writing "sorry your crown fell out" confirms the reviewer is a patient and references treatment — a HIPAA problem. This prompt is built to never do either.

Prompt 3 · No-show recovery

Re-book a no-show without making them feel judged

Use when: a patient missed their appointment without cancelling.

Write a brief, non-judgmental follow-up message to a dental patient who
missed their appointment at [PRACTICE NAME] on [DATE] at [TIME]. Express
that we're still here for them, invite them to reschedule, and mention our
cancellation policy ([POLICY, e.g., "24-hour notice required"]) in a warm,
not punitive way. Under 100 words. Can be sent as email or SMS.

Why it works: a defensive no-show message loses the patient for good. This one recovers the appointment while still stating the policy.
